# Context pack: house voice

## What a runner can rely on from this pack

We write the way an experienced studio owner talks to a newer one over coffee: specific,
unsentimental, and willing to say what did not work. We are not a brand with opinions
about wellness; we are people who have watched 4,000 studios try things.

Rules that survive every edit:

1. **Name the number or drop the claim.** "Most studios" is a placeholder for a number
   somebody has to go find.
2. **Second person, present tense.** "You raise prices in January and lose the members you
   least wanted to lose."
3. **No throat-clearing.** The first sentence carries information. Delete any opening that
   describes the industry.
4. **Concede the strongest counter-argument in the piece**, not in a footnote.
5. **British spelling, Oxford comma, no exclamation marks.**

## Worked examples

| Situation | Good | Not good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening a churn piece | "Six of every ten cancellations at FIY studios happen in week three." | "In today's competitive fitness landscape, retention is more important than ever." | The first sentence is either information or it is filler |
| Citing product data | "Studios using the save-desk keep 12% more members in month two (n=340, Q2)." | "Our data shows the save-desk really works." | A number with an n, or nothing |
| Disagreeing with a trend | "Class-pack pricing works, and it will cost you your best instructors. Here is the trade." | "Some argue class packs have downsides." | Take the position, then pay for it |

## Boundaries

This pack does not cover: enterprise sales copy (different reader, different pack),
in-product microcopy, or anything with legal exposure. Escalate to Dana.
