# Context pack: audience map

## What a runner can rely on from this pack

**Primary reader**: owner-operator of 1–5 studio locations, teaches some classes, does the
books on Sunday night. Reads on a phone between classes. Has been sold to relentlessly and
trusts peers over vendors.

**What they already believe** (do not spend words re-establishing these):

- Retention matters more than acquisition. They know. They do not know what to *do*.
- Software is a cost centre until it visibly saves an hour.
- Consultants overpromise.

**What they disbelieve, and why it matters**: that any number from a vendor is real. This
is why the "name the number, name the n" rule exists — it is credibility, not style.

**Representative respondents for a probe**: studio owners in the community who post about
operations, not about marketing. Peer-group operators at 2–5 locations. *Not*
representative: our champions on customer calls, our own staff, agency partners.

## Worked examples

| Situation | Good | Not good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing an angle | "What to do in week three, when they go quiet" | "The importance of member retention" | They have the belief; they lack the move |
| Probing an angle | Post the question in the ops thread, count replies that describe their own week three | Ask our three friendliest customers if it is interesting | Friendly ≠ representative |

## Boundaries

Enterprise buyers (6+ locations, procurement, security reviews) are a different reader with
a different pack. If a piece is for them, escalate — do not stretch this one.

## Provenance & freshness

- **How we know it has rotted**: a `learn` readout that contradicts a belief listed here.
  Two of those and this pack gets rewritten, not patched.
- **Review cadence**: after every fourth learn readout.
