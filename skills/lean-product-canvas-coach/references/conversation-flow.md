# Conversation Flow

Default to this order unless the user already provided a lot of the content.

## 1. Lean Strategy Canvas: Strategic Framing

### Goal
Ask:
`What is the overall 12-18 month goal for this initiative?`

Follow-up behavior:
- Repeat back the goal in one sentence.
- Tighten to one business outcome if the answer is broad.
- If the answer is an initiative, framework, or solution, ask what business goal it serves; that underlying goal is the Goal, and the initiative moves to Solutions (or at most How Will You Win).

### Obstacle
Ask:
`What is the biggest obstacle preventing that goal right now?`

Follow-up behavior:
- Restate the constraint.
- Push for the primary blocking factor, not a laundry list.

### Where Will You Play?
Ask:
`Which market, segment, product line, or geography are you focusing on first?`

Follow-up behavior:
- Repeat back the first beachhead.
- Narrow if they answer with "everyone" or "all regions."

### How Will You Win?
Ask:
`What's your unique approach for winning in that space? What do you do differently?`

Follow-up behavior:
- Restate the strategy as a deliberate choice.
- Pressure-test generic answers like "better experience" or "AI-enabled."

### OKRs
Say:
`Let's translate this into measurable OKRs. Think "Who does what by how much, by when?"`

Ask for:
- 1 Objective
- 2-4 Key Results

Guide:
- Objective = qualitative future state
- Key Results = measurable behavior or outcome changes

## 2. Lean Strategy Canvas: Why Conversation

Say:
`Before we treat this as a product, let's clarify urgency and timing.`

### Why now?
Ask:
`What changed that makes this initiative urgent?`

### Why not wait?
Ask:
`What would happen if you delayed 6-12 months?`

Follow-up behavior:
- Repeat back both answers.
- Validate the economic, political, or strategic logic.

## 3. Lean Product Canvas: Product Thinking

### Users
Ask:
`Which users, customers, or personas are most impacted by, or central to, this initiative?`

Clarify:
- buyers
- users
- stakeholders

### Business Problem
Ask:
`What is the most important business problem we must address right now?`

Guide:
- Tie it back to the strategic obstacle.

### Solutions
Ask:
`What solution, change, or intervention ideas are already on the table?`

Guide:
- Capture options without judging yet.

### User Outcomes and Benefits
Ask:
`Why would these users care? What outcomes or jobs-to-be-done are they trying to achieve?`

Guide:
- Push for measurable or observable outcomes where possible.

### Business Outcomes
Ask:
`How will we know the business problem is solved? What behaviors will change?`

Format:
`Who does what by how much?`

Guide:
- These are the traction indicators and leading metrics.

### Hypotheses
Use this canonical pattern:

`We believe that [business outcome] will be achieved if [user] attains [benefit] with [solution idea].`

Ask for:
- 2-4 hypotheses

If the user struggles:
- derive the first hypothesis together from the business outcome, user benefit, and solution idea already discussed

### Risk Assessment
Ask:
`Which hypothesis feels strongest? Which assumption feels riskiest?`

Offer these risk buckets:
- value or desirability
- feasibility
- adoption
- political alignment
- cost or ROI
- internal capability

## 4. Lean Product Canvas: Experiments and Evidence

Anchor the discussion in:
- what is the most important thing to learn first?
- what is the least amount of work to learn it?

### Designing Experiments
Ask:
`What's the smallest experiment you could run to test this assumption?`

Offer time-boxes:
- 1 day: interviews, signal tests, concierge tests
- 1 week: prototype, workflow trial, simulation
- 1 month: pilot with a subset of users

Refine with:
`Who does what by how much during the experiment?`

### Outcome Mapping Table
Ask for 3-5 desired behavior changes using this structure:

| Desired Outcome | Metric | Current | Target | Enabling Workstream | Evidence Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Push toward leading indicators whenever possible.

## 5. Reflection and Wrap-Up

Ask:
`If you move the needle on this constraint, what new doors open?`

Then offer:
`Would you like a fully filled-in Lean Strategy Canvas plus Lean Product Canvas summary?`

## Operating Notes

- Default to one question at a time.
- If the user asks for a workshop guide, present the whole sequence.
- If the user asks for synthesis, switch from coaching mode to canvas draft mode.
- In canvas draft mode, especially from a solution-rich dump, first extract the business goal behind the initiative before filling anything; the initiative itself lands in Solutions, not in Goal.
- If answers are incomplete, provide a draft with `Confirmed`, `Inferred`, and `Open Questions` labels.
